Law and Criminality in Colombia

Case Study: The "False Positives" Scandal
👤 Name: _______________ 📅 Date: 23.10.2026 🏫 Subject: Law and Criminality

1 Choose a Country

Country: 🇨🇴 Colombia (South America)

Why I chose this country:
  • Colombia had a war for more than 50 years.
  • Now Colombia has a special court for war crimes.
  • This case shows why the law must protect ordinary people.
Flag of Colombia (Wikimedia Commons)

2 Research a Crime

The crime: "False Positives"
(in Spanish: "Falsos Positivos")

Simple meaning:
  • Colombian soldiers killed innocent people (people who did nothing wrong).
  • Then they said: "These people were dangerous fighters."
  • But it was a lie. The killed people were innocent.

Time: 2002 – 2008

Real photo: people in Colombia protest for the victims (Wikimedia Commons)

3 What Happened?

  • Soldiers took poor and simple people to far places.
  • They killed them.
  • They put guns near the dead bodies.
  • They told their bosses: "We caught the bad guys!"
  • For every dead body, soldiers got money and days off.
Result: more than 6,400 innocent people were killed.
Colombian soldiers, 2005 (Wikimedia Commons)

4 Why Did It Happen?

1. Pressure 💨
The army wanted to show big results — and fast.
2. Money 💵
Soldiers got a cash gift for every "bad guy" they killed.
3. No control 👁️
The big bosses did not check the information.
4. The war ⚔️
Colombia fought illegal armed groups for many years, so fear was very big.

5 How Did It Happen?

  • Step 1. Commanders said: "This month you must catch X bad guys."
  • Step 2. Soldiers could not find the real bad guys.
  • Step 3. So they chose innocent people: poor workers, homeless people, sick people.
  • Step 4. They killed them and wrote fake reports.
  • Step 5. Finally lawyers and journalists found the truth.
Key word: "Fake report" = a paper that says something, but it is not true.

6 What Was the Consequence?

"Consequence" = what happened after the crime — the results.

For the families 💔
Thousands of mothers, wives and children lost their men.
For the army 🎖️
People stopped trusting the soldiers.
For Colombia 🇨🇴
A big scandal, many street protests.
For the world 🌍
Big groups like the UN and Human Rights Watch spoke about it.

7 What Was the Punishment?

Law used: the Criminal Code of Colombia
("Código Penal", law Ley 599 of the year 2000)
  • The crime name: murder done together with a group.
  • Many soldiers and officers were sent to prison.
  • Some generals got 25 to 40 years in prison.
  • The state also paid money to the families (this is called compensation — money to say "sorry").

8 The Reason for the Punishment

Rule 1. The Constitution of Colombia (year 1991) says:
"Every person has the right to live." (Article 11)
Rule 2. Killing an innocent person is one of the worst crimes.
Rule 3. A soldier works for the people — so a soldier must protect people, not kill them.
Rule 4. The punishment was strong because the crime was done many times, on purpose.

9 Was the Punishment Effective?

"Effective" = did it really work? Did the crime stop?

✅ Yes — good results:
  • After 2008, this crime almost stopped.
  • New rules: bosses must now check every report.
  • Families got some justice and money.
❌ No — still problems:
  • Some big bosses still did not get a strong punishment.
  • Many families are still waiting for the full truth.

10 Conclusion

What does the law say about this crime?

  • In Colombia, killing a person is a crime — and it is still a crime even in war time.
  • Colombia created a special court for such crimes: the JEP
    (full name: "Jurisdicción Especial para la Paz" = Special Court for Peace)
  • Lesson: no soldier is above the law. The law must protect every person.
